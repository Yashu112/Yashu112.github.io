A majority coloring of a graph is a vertex coloring in which each
vertex has at most half of its neighbors sharing its color.
Lovász showed in 1966 that two colors always suffice for any finite
graph.
In the majority coloring game, two players alternately color vertices
while maintaining the majority condition. The least number of colors
for which the first player has a winning strategy is the majority game
chromatic number $\mu_g(G)$.
While this number is unbounded for general graphs, it is at most four
for trees. We study $\mu_g(G)$ using a configuration-based
strategy that prevents a small family of local obstruction patterns.

An interactive [Tree Coloring Explorer]({{ '/game/' | relative_url }})
is available for experimenting with majority colorings on trees.
